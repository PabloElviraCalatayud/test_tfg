\chapter*{Abstract}
Gait analysis is a diagnostic tool with applications ranging from clinical medicine to sports performance. The way a person steps while walking, running, or performing any similar activity provides highly valuable physical metrics for analyzing alterations in movement patterns. Changes in pressure distribution, temperature, or dynamic stability may indicate pathologies such as diabetes or Parkinson’s disease, whose early detection has a direct impact on both quality of life and treatment costs.

In recent decades, advances in so-called wearables have brought about a radical transformation in the way physiological and biomechanical parameters are monitored, exponentially driving telemedicine by enabling continuous patient monitoring outside clinical environments. Low-power embedded systems, low-energy wireless communication protocols, and cloud-based IoT platforms have made it possible to capture, transmit, and analyze data in real time from virtually any environment.

Dynamic gait analysis refers to the measurement of a set of variables during a subject’s actual movement. It requires a system capable of synchronously acquiring plantar pressure data, surface temperature, and information regarding the inertial behavior of the foot. This set of variables enables characterization of both load distribution and foot kinematics, providing a complete picture of the user’s gait cycle.

Despite the clinical and sports relevance of dynamic gait analysis, currently available solutions present accessibility barriers that limit their widespread use. Laboratory systems based on pressure platforms and camera systems used to analyze movement remain confined to specialized environments due to their cost and infrastructure requirements. Instrumented insoles designed for medical applications often depend on specific equipment, such as ankle braces or orthopedic boots, reducing accessibility. Finally, the more autonomous solutions currently available on the market are expensive and therefore reserved for professional applications. At present, no solution combines full portability, operational autonomy, and low cost within a single device.

This work presents the design, development, and validation of both a device and an IoT platform for dynamic gait monitoring. The data acquisition device consists of 12 force-sensitive resistors, 4 thermistors, and a 9-degree-of-freedom IMU attached to a 3D-printed insole covered with EVA foam, along with an ESP32-S3 microcontroller located in a 3D-printed ankle support responsible for managing data acquisition and transmission. The collected data are transmitted in real time using NimBLE to an application where they can be visualized as heat maps or through an artificial horizon used to indicate the relative position of the foot during gait. The IoT platform is completed with a full IoT data pipeline that includes an MQTT broker, persistent storage in PostgreSQL, and data visualization in Grafana.

\begin{IAtext}
The development of the system has jointly addressed the acquisition device and the software platform that accompanies it. In the ESP32-S3 firmware, priority has been given to ensuring that the transmitted data is always the RAW reading from the analog-to-digital converter and the IMU registers, without any conversion to physical units before transmission; this decision moves all calibration logic to the mobile application, making it possible to adjust full-scale ranges, force-sensitive resistor (FSR) conversion curves, or the coefficients of the Steinhart-Hart equation used for the thermistors without having to reprogram the microcontroller. The BLE communication protocol, built on top of NimBLE, has been designed with an explicit version byte in the header of every packet, so that any mismatch between the firmware version and the application version is detected and logged explicitly instead of manifesting as a silent loss of packets. In addition, work has been carried out on the energy efficiency of the device through dynamic CPU frequency scaling, the use of low-power modes (Bluetooth controller \textit{light-sleep} and \textit{modem-sleep}), and an event-driven task architecture instead of periodic polling, reducing the number of unnecessary BLE notifications. On the mobile application side, developed in Flutter, a foreground service has been incorporated on Android, together with the background execution modes provided by iOS, so that the Bluetooth connection remains active when the application is no longer in the foreground, along with an automatic reconnection strategy. Local persistence of the data has also been added by means of an embedded SQLite database, allowing the user to consult their daily step history without depending on the availability of the MQTT broker or the cloud platform.
\end{IAtext}

\begin{IAtext}
Compared with the solutions described in the state of the art, this work is positioned as an alternative that combines low component cost, full portability, and operational autonomy within a single device, aspects that rarely coexist in existing commercial or laboratory solutions. Unlike laboratory pressure platforms, the developed system does not require a fixed installation or a controlled environment. Unlike medically oriented instrumented insoles, it does not depend on ankle braces or other additional orthopedic equipment beyond the 3D-printed support that houses the microcontroller itself. And unlike high-end autonomous solutions aimed at professional sports, its material cost remains low thanks to the use of low-cost FSR sensors and thermistors, 3D printing for the mechanical structure, and general-purpose electronic components (ESP32-S3, ADS1115, LSM9DS1).
\end{IAtext}

The obtained results demonstrate the feasibility of building an autonomous and low-cost dynamic gait analysis system. The integration of the complete pipeline, from data acquisition to cloud visualization, opens the possibility of democratizing this type of analysis beyond laboratory environments and extending it to primary healthcare settings, rehabilitation clinics, and remote monitoring for athletes.

Beyond validating the developed system, the capabilities for continuous data acquisition and storage create new opportunities to expand the platform’s functional scope. The main future line of work is the incorporation of an artificial intelligence model for the automatic analysis and interpretation of the data, serving as a support tool for both professionals and non-specialized end users.
